How to make an inventory management app in AppSheet

Build a working inventory app on Google AppSheet: products with photos and barcodes, a stock in/out log, live stock counts and low-stock alerts. No coding, just a few short formulas you can copy.

By Joe Tayactac, AppSheet developer with 100+ apps built

What you'll build

  • A product catalog with photos, categories and barcodes
  • A stock in/out log that records who moved what, and when
  • Live stock counts calculated by the app, so they never drift
  • A low-stock list, red highlights and an email alert
  • Barcode scanning from any phone

1Set up your Google Sheet

Create a new Google Sheet with two tabs. The first holds your products, the second records every time stock comes in or goes out. Put the column names in row 1 and keep the sheet clean: no merged cells, no blank columns between headers, no totals rows.

Tab 1: Products

Product ID, Name, Category, Barcode, Photo, Unit, Reorder Level

Tab 2: Stock Movements

Movement ID, Date, Product, Type, Quantity, Location, Note

Don't add a "Current Stock" column you type into. If people overwrite a number by hand, it drifts away from reality and nobody knows why. In this design, AppSheet calculates the stock from the movements, so every unit can be traced back to who moved it and when.

2Create the app from the sheet

  1. In your Google Sheet, open Extensions and choose AppSheet, then Create an app.
  2. AppSheet reads the first tab and builds a starter app. In the AppSheet editor, open Data and add the Stock Movements tab as a second table.
  3. Allow adds, updates and deletes on both tables, so staff can record movements from the app.

3Set the column types

Open each table's columns and set them like this. The types matter: they decide which input people see and how AppSheet connects the two tables.

Products

ColumnTypeSettings
Product IDTextKey. Initial value UNIQUEID(). Hide it from users.
NameTextLabel, so products show by name everywhere.
CategoryEnumAdd your categories. Allow other values if the list grows.
BarcodeTextTurn on Scannable so phones can scan into it.
PhotoImageOptional, but makes picking the right item much faster.
UnitEnumpcs, box, kg, and so on.
Reorder LevelNumberThe stock level that should trigger a reorder.

Stock Movements

ColumnTypeSettings
Movement IDTextKey. Initial value UNIQUEID(). Hidden.
DateDateTimeInitial value NOW().
ProductRefSource table: Products. Required.
TypeEnumTwo values, exactly In and Out.
QuantityNumberRequired. Always a positive number; Type decides the direction.
LocationEnumYour stores, vans or warehouses.
NoteLongTextOptional: supplier, job number, reason.
Why the Ref matters: once Product is a Ref to Products, AppSheet automatically adds a list column to Products called Related Stock Movements. That list is what the stock count in the next step adds up.

4Calculate live stock with virtual columns

Add two virtual columns. Virtual columns are calculated by AppSheet and don't need a column in the sheet.

In Stock Movements: Signed Qty (Number)

Turns every movement into a plus or minus number:

App formula: Signed Qty
IF([Type] = "In", [Quantity], -[Quantity])

In Products: Current Stock (Number)

Adds up all movements for that product:

App formula: Current Stock
SUM([Related Stock Movements][Signed Qty])

In Products: Stock Status (Text)

Optional, but handy for colors and filters:

App formula: Stock Status
IFS(
  [Current Stock] <= 0, "Out of stock",
  [Current Stock] <= [Reorder Level], "Low",
  TRUE, "OK"
)
Starting from an existing count? Add one movement per product with Type In and today's quantity. From then on, the history is complete.

5Build the views your team will use

  1. Products: a Deck or Gallery view showing Photo, Name and Current Stock.
  2. Stock In / Out: a Form view on Stock Movements, so staff can log a movement in a few taps.
  3. Low Stock: create a slice on Products with this row filter, then a Table view on the slice:
Slice row filter: Low Stock
[Current Stock] <= [Reorder Level]

Add a format rule on Products with the same condition and a red color, so low items stand out in every view. If you like, finish with a Dashboard view that combines Low Stock and the latest movements.

6Add barcode scanning

The fastest setup, if every product has a unique barcode, is to make the barcode the key:

  1. In Products, set Barcode as the Key instead of Product ID.
  2. In Stock Movements, turn on Scannable for the Product column.
  3. Now staff open the Stock In / Out form, tap the scan icon, point the camera at the barcode, and the product is picked for them.
Only switch the key early. Changing a table's key after you already have records breaks the links between existing rows. Decide before you start entering real data. If some products have no barcode, keep Product ID as the key and use the search box instead.

7Email yourself when stock runs low

  1. Open Automation and create a new bot.
  2. Event: a data change on Stock Movements, for Adds only.
  3. Add this condition, so the bot only runs when a movement takes a product to or below its reorder level:
Bot condition
[Product].[Current Stock] <= [Product].[Reorder Level]
  1. Task: Send an email to yourself or your purchasing person, with a subject like:
Email subject
Low stock: <<[Product].[Name]>> (<<[Product].[Current Stock]>> left)

8Test it, then share it

  1. Add a few test products and log some In and Out movements. Check that Current Stock and the Low Stock list change the way you expect.
  2. Delete the test rows, enter your real opening stock as In movements, and share the app with your team using the Share button. Each person signs in with their Google account.
  3. Watch how people use it for a week, then improve it. Real use always shows what's missing.

Common issues

Stuck on something else? Use the Troubleshoot button at the top and tell me what's happening.

Current Stock shows 0 or stays empty

Check three things: the Product column in Stock Movements is a Ref to Products, the Signed Qty virtual column exists, and the Type values are exactly In and Out. A Type of in or IN won't match the formula.

I can't find Related Stock Movements

AppSheet only creates that list when the Product column is a Ref with Products as its source table. If you just changed the column type, save, and make sure the column in the sheet holds product keys, not names.

A product picked by scanning doesn't match

Scanning fills the Ref with the scanned text, so it only matches when the barcode is the key of Products. Either make Barcode the key, or pick the product from the list instead of scanning.

The numbers changed after I edited the sheet directly

Edits made in Google Sheets reach the app on the next sync, and Current Stock is recalculated from the movements. Record changes as movements in the app rather than editing old rows, so the history stays correct.

The app gets slow as the movement history grows

Virtual columns that add up long lists are recalculated as data syncs, so very large tables slow down. Once you have many thousands of movements, archive older rows into a separate sheet and add one opening-balance movement per product.
